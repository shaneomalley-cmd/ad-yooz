What Is Accounts Payable Automation?

Accounts payable automation is software that replaces the manual steps of paying a business's bills — receiving invoices, checking them against a purchase order, routing them for sign-off, and paying the vendor — with a digital, rules-driven process. Instead of a person keying invoice data by hand and walking a paper approval form around an office, the software captures the invoice, reads its data automatically, matches it to what was ordered, and sends it to the right approver — with a full audit trail attached to every invoice from receipt to payment.

How AP automation software works

Every AP automation platform is organized around the same underlying sequence, whichever vendor builds it. Yooz names the six stages plainly:

  1. 1Purchase
  2. →
  3. 2Capture
  4. →
  5. 3Review
  6. →
  7. 4Approve
  8. →
  9. 5Pay
  10. →
  11. 6Export
  • Purchase — a purchase order is raised and approved before the spend happens, so there's something for the invoice to be checked against later.
  • Capture — the invoice arrives and its data is pulled out automatically, however it shows up: email, drag & drop, mobile, scan, sFTP, e-invoicing, fetcher or API.
  • Review — the software checks the invoice against the purchase order and receipt (a "3-way match") and flags anything that doesn't line up.
  • Approve — the invoice routes itself to the right approver based on rules — amount, department, vendor — instead of a person deciding where it goes next.
  • Pay — payment is scheduled and released on the terms agreed with the vendor.
  • Export — the transaction posts back into the company's accounting or ERP system, so the general ledger stays current without a second manual entry.

The point of automating this sequence isn't to remove people from AP — it's to remove the manual, repetitive parts of it (typing, filing, chasing a paper form) so the humans left in the process are making judgment calls, not data entry.

What is AP software, specifically?

"AP software" and "accounts payable automation software" are the same category of product, described two ways. It's the application a finance team logs into to see every invoice's status, approve or query one, and check what's scheduled to be paid — rather than the automation running invisibly in the background. In practice, the two are one piece of software: the interface and the automation behind it.

Agentic AI and autonomous AP

"Agentic AI accounts payable" and "autonomous accounts payable" describe the newest layer on top of the process above: software that doesn't just route work to a person, but takes some of the Review and Approve decisions itself, within rules a finance team sets — matching an invoice, resolving a small discrepancy, or approving a payment under a defined threshold without a human touching it.

It's a matter of degree, not a different process. The same six stages still run; autonomous AP simply narrows how much of Review and Approve still needs a human, and widens what the software is trusted to decide on its own.
